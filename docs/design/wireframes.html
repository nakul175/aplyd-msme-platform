APLYD MSME platform · design documents

Wireframes of the screens as built

Version 0.2 · 5 October 2026 · Draft, not reviewed by a person

These drawings show the screens of the APLYD MSME platform as they were built in the interface redesign (slice 10). What they show was observed on 5 October 2026 on the application running with synthetic demo data, at a phone width of 390 pixels and a desktop width of 1,280 pixels, and was read against the source code. No owner of a factory has used these screens. Every company, person, provider and figure on them is SYNTHETIC.

The words on headings, tags, buttons and labels are the words on the pages. Long paragraphs are drawn as grey bars. Nothing here is a screenshot. The text version of these drawings, with the same numbers, is WIREFRAMES.md; every field, message and state is in section 5 of the functional specification, which uses the same screen numbers.

Key to the drawings

The drawings use greys and one accent colour. They follow the light or dark setting of the browser. They are low fidelity: a box is where something is, not how it looks.

Banner, app bar, footer

A bar: the banner, the app bar or the footer.

A card

A card: one block of a page.

A highlighted card

A highlighted card, drawn with the one accent colour: it is the owner's turn, the sign-off, or what comes next.

Not always there

A dashed box: it is drawn only in some states, or it is a closed section that the owner can open.
Grey bars: a paragraph of text that is not written out here. The words are in the functional specification.

SCR-01 Home

Tells a visitor what the service is; tells an owner the next step and where each of the five steps stands; gives the operator the way to the provider list.

A visitor, not signed in

Drawn from: Observed in the capture (a visitor); read in app/page.tsx.

  1. 1
    Opening block. The service in one line, a short paragraph, and the one button "Sign in" (to the sign-in page), with "You are not signed in." beside it. It is the only "Sign in" link on the page. The top heading is the name of the service. FR-UX-22, FR-ID-12
  2. 2
    How it works. Five steps in plain words, one line each: the one-word name of the step in bold and what the step is. From 52 rem wide the five sit in a row. FR-UX-22
  3. 3
    What you can rely on. Four lines, each a bold sentence and one more: nothing is overwritten (every save is kept as a version); other companies cannot see yours (the rule is kept on the server); you decide (ideas are ranked by fixed rules and nothing goes on without your yes); no money moves here. FR-UX-22
  4. 4
    About this version. Says it is an early version, in English only, for one factory at a time, with no photos or voice yet, and that nothing is a finding about a real factory. FR-UX-22

An owner whose plan is agreed and who has no starting number

Drawn from: Observed in the capture (owner D, the company with an agreed plan); read in app/page.tsx and modules/journey/src/progress.ts.

  1. 1
    The next step. The line "Your next step", the title as the top heading, one sentence and one button. All four come from the one rule of `nextStep` that fires: thirteen rules tried in order, the first that fits wins (FSD, the table under FR-UX-20). Here it is the rule `record_baseline`. The button goes to the step's page (here Results), not to the control that does the step. FR-UX-20, FR-UX-22
  2. 2
    Signed in as. The display name and the role, as a tag. FR-UX-22
  3. 3
    Your five steps. The heading of the list below. FR-UX-22
  4. 4
    Five stage cards. Each has a dot (the step's number, or a tick when done), a link named for the page (Describe your factory, What to do about it, What you need, Delivery, Results), a tag (Done, Your turn or Not yet), the stage's sentence (drawn as a grey bar here) and a line saying where it stands. The card of the step in note 1 is "Your turn". Here Delivery is "Not yet" although a plan is agreed, because a stage is Done only when an engagement is complete (FR-UX-21). FR-UX-21
  5. 5
    Company record. The company name as a heading, the tag SYNTHETIC for a synthetic company, and the date the record was created. FR-UX-22, FR-ID-12
  6. 6
    What you can rely on. The same four lines as for a visitor. FR-UX-22

Other states of this view

  • An owner who has done nothing: the step is "Start by describing your factory", the first card is "Your turn" and the others "Not yet" with lines such as "Waits for a confirmed description".
  • An owner who is up to date: "You are up to date" with the button "Look back at the numbers"; no card is marked "Your turn".
  • The thirteen rules and their words are in the FSD under FR-UX-20.

Wide screens. From 52 rem wide the five cards are on the left and the two cards on the right, as drawn in the wide view. Below that they are one column, in the order of the text drawing.

The operator

  1. 1
    Opening block. A fixed block, not a next step: the operator has no steps. It says what the operator keeps (providers and one listing fee), that the fee is the same for every provider, and that the operator sees providers and counts, never a company's record. One button, to the operator page. FR-UX-22
  2. 2
    Signed in as. Display name and role. FR-UX-22
  3. 3

Other states of this view

SCR-02 · /sign-in · app/sign-in/page.tsx · FSD section 5.3

SCR-02 Sign-in

Lets a person choose one of the synthetic users and sign in as that user. There is no password.

The sign-in page

Drawn from: Observed in the capture (a visitor); read in app/sign-in/page.tsx.

  1. 1
    Head. The title "Sign in" and one sentence: "Synthetic environment: choose a synthetic user. There is no password." There is no step line, no five-step strip and no next step on this page. FR-UX-16, FR-ID-01
  2. 2
    Refusal. Drawn only after a refused sign-in (the address ends with refused=1): "That user was not found." It is announced as an alert. FR-ID-02, MSG-05
  3. 3
    The one field. A drop-down list of the synthetic users, one for each, shown as "display name (role)" and ordered by name; the value sent is the email address. It is required. The capture lists eight: the operator and owners A to G. FR-ID-01
  4. 4
    The button. Posts to /api/session. Success goes to the home page; a refusal comes back to this page. If the session secret is missing the form is still shown and the post answers a line of JSON instead of a page (FSD section 12, item 16). FR-ID-02
  5. 5
    Who is who. Two entries. Owner: describes a factory and works through the five steps, and sees that company and no other. Operator: keeps the list of providers and the listing fee, and never sees a company's record. FR-ID-01

Other states of this view

  • Where the environment is not synthetic or no database is bound, the page holds only the title and "Sign-in is not available in this environment." (MSG-04).

SCR-03 · /factory · app/factory/page.tsx · FSD section 5.4

SCR-03 Describe your factory

Lets the owner answer the questionnaire, save it as a version, confirm a version as accurate and look at earlier versions.

An owner whose description is confirmed

Drawn from: Observed in the capture (owner A, a confirmed description); read in app/factory/page.tsx.

  1. 1
    The five steps. Describe is done and is the page the owner is on (underlined); Decide is the owner's turn. Each is a link. Drawn on each stage page for an owner of a company, and not for a visitor or a person who is not an owner. FR-UX-17
  2. 2
    Head. "Step 1 of 5 · Describe", the title as the one top heading, and one sentence: "Answer what you can and stop whenever you like: every save is kept as a new version, and nothing is overwritten." FR-UX-16
  3. 3
    Where your description stands. A tag for the state (Confirmed with a tick, Draft, or Not started) and the tag SYNTHETIC; one sentence; a bar with its words; and, for a saved version that is not confirmed, a line saying how many of the five questions needed to confirm are answered. The count is of what was saved, so it does not move while the person types. FR-UX-23, FR-DESC-04
  4. 4
    The questions. 25 questions in five section cards (23 always apply and two ask "If something else, what?"). A question with several choices is a group whose legend is its label. The five questions needed to confirm carry the tag "Needed to confirm". The browser requires nothing; the server checks. A radio choice, once made, cannot be cleared on the page. FR-DESC-01, FR-DESC-02
  5. 5
    The other four sections. How work gets done; What you use today; What is hurting most (choose up to three); What you can spend and decide. Drawn as titles only here. FR-DESC-01
  6. 6
    The note. "What changed since last time? (optional)", up to 500 characters, and a sentence saying what confirming means. FR-DESC-03
  7. 7
    Two buttons. "Save as draft" and "Confirm this is accurate" (the main button), in a bar that stays at the bottom of the screen while the owner scrolls (not on a screen lower than 34 rem); on a phone they share one row. Both post a new version. Confirming works only when the five questions are answered. A post made from a version that is no longer the newest is refused. FR-DESC-03, FR-DESC-04, FR-DESC-05
  8. 8
    Earlier versions. Closed when the page loads; open while an older version is read. Each version is a link, and an older version cannot be changed. FR-DESC-06
  9. 9
    Your next step. Drawn because the owner's turn is on another stage. One title, one sentence (not drawn here) and one button to that stage. It is left out when the turn is on this page. FR-UX-18, FR-UX-20

Other states of this view

  • Nothing saved: the tag "Not started", "Nothing saved yet." and the help line "A draft can be saved after answering just one question."
  • A draft: the tag "Draft" and the count of the five questions needed to confirm.
  • An older version is being read: a notice "You are looking at version N ... It cannot be changed." and the form is read-only.
  • Refused or saved: notices for the codes saved, confirmed, missing, problem and stale (MSG-19 to MSG-46).
  • A visitor, or a person who is not an owner: the title and one card with a sentence; no strip.

SCR-04 What to do about it

Lets the owner make an assessment from the confirmed description, read the ranked ideas, decide on each, and sign the assessment off.

The first visit, before an assessment exists

Drawn from: Observed in the capture (owner A); read in app/diagnose/page.tsx.

  1. 1
    The make card. A large card until an assessment exists from the confirmed version. It has no field: one button makes the assessment from the newest confirmed description by fixed rules. A person with no confirmed description sees a card with a large link "Describe your factory" instead. FR-DIAG-01, FR-DIAG-05
  2. 2
    The list. "None yet." until the first assessment exists. FR-DIAG-10

An assessment, two of five ideas decided

Drawn from: Read in app/diagnose/page.tsx and modules/diagnose/src/progress.ts. The capture shows only the first visit and the signed-off assessment, so this state was not seen in the capture.

  1. 1
    The five steps. Decide is the owner's turn and the page the owner is on. FR-UX-17
  2. 2
    Head. "Step 2 of 5 · Decide", the title "What to do about it" and one sentence: "Here is a short, ranked list of ideas for your factory: for each one you say yes, not yet or no, and then sign off." FR-UX-16
  3. 3
    The SYNTHETIC line. The tag and one sentence: the ideas and every figure are placeholders written to test the service, not advice and not estimates. It is not in a closed section. FR-UX-01, FR-UX-24
  4. 4
    The assessment. The heading names the version of the description it was made from; an assessment made from an older version carries the tag "Older version". Then "Drafted by a set of rules ... not by an AI model" with the library and engine versions, and the problems the owner named. FR-DIAG-01, FR-DIAG-05, FR-DIAG-06
  5. 5
    The sign-off card. A bar with its words, one sentence saying what is left, a link to the next idea with no decision, and the button "Sign off this assessment". The button is disabled until there is at least one idea and every idea has a decision; the sentence on what signing off means is then in a closed section. Once every idea is decided the card also shows a note field and the button becomes the main button. FR-DIAG-08, FR-UX-23
  6. 6
    The ideas, ranked. The heading above one card for each idea, best first. FR-DIAG-02
  7. 7
    An idea with a decision. Rank and title; tags for the decision, for AI or no AI, for readiness and for risk; the summary; what it could do, the one-time cost, the cost every year after, and the first year in all with a comparison to the owner's yearly budget; what to do first and what to check; hidden costs; the risk; why it is here; a closed section "Source and score"; then the form: three radio choices (accept, later, no), a note of up to 500 characters and a button that saves that one decision. On a screen 62 rem wide or more the form is a column beside the idea. FR-DIAG-04, FR-DIAG-07
  8. 8
    The first idea with no decision. The tag "Not decided yet" and a main button on the save button of this idea. The page marks one main button at a time. FR-DIAG-07, FR-UX-23
  9. 9
    Closed sections. "How the ideas were ranked" (the points for each term) and "Ideas left out (N)" (each with the reason). FR-DIAG-03, FR-DIAG-06, FR-UX-24
  10. 10
    Your assessments. One link for each assessment, with the tag "Shown above" on the one on view, and a quiet button to make an assessment again from the confirmed version (the same version gives the same assessment). FR-DIAG-10, FR-DIAG-01

Other states of this view

  • No ideas match the problems the owner named: a notice, the sign-off button stays disabled and a sign-off is refused.
  • An unknown assessment in the address (?a=) shows no assessment and no message (FSD section 12, item 6).
  • A person who is a member of the company, not the owner, is told that only the owner can make and sign off an assessment (MSG-48).

Wide screens. From 62 rem wide each idea has its decision form in a column beside it.

An assessment that is signed off

Drawn from: Observed in the capture (owner C, where a delivery waits); read in app/diagnose/page.tsx.

  1. 1
    Signed off. The heading "Signed off" with a tick, the date, the owner's note if any, the bar, and the first 16 characters of the fingerprint with the result of checking it against what is stored just now. When the owner's turn is on a later step, "Next: <title>" and that step's button are in this card, and the card "Your next step" is left out of the foot of the page so the step is not shown twice. FR-DIAG-08, FR-UX-18
  2. 2
    Ideas, no forms. After the sign-off each idea shows the decision as a tag and in words, and has no form: nothing on a signed-off assessment can be changed. FR-DIAG-07, FR-DIAG-08

SCR-05 What you need

Lets the owner turn an accepted idea into a requirement in a cluster, see how many companies ask for the same, see the providers listed for it, choose one, or withdraw the requirement.

An owner with two requirements, one still without a provider

Drawn from: Observed in the capture (owner D); read in app/procure/page.tsx and modules/procure/src/view.ts.

  1. 1
    The five steps. Choose is the page; the owner's turn is Measure (the starting number). FR-UX-17
  2. 2
    Head. "Step 3 of 5 · Choose", the title "What you need" and one sentence: "Say what you need for each idea you accepted, then choose a provider." FR-UX-16
  3. 3
    The SYNTHETIC line. The tag and: the ideas, the clusters and the providers are placeholders; no quote, price, contract or payment exists here; this only records what you are asking for. FR-UX-01
  4. 4
    Where this stands. Drawn when an idea is accepted or a requirement exists. One sentence saying what is next (grey bar here), tags for the counts (marigold in the application for the one that is the owner's turn), a bar with its words and, when a provider is to be chosen and an idea is ready, a link to the requirements. FR-UX-23
  5. 5
    Ideas you accepted. The ideas the owner said yes to in the newest signed-off assessment. "Ready to ask for" holds those with no requirement yet. FR-PROC-01
  6. 6
    Make a requirement. One card for each idea without a requirement: title, tags, summary, the cost from the assessment (a placeholder), and one field, the cluster, with a drop-down of three SYNTHETIC clusters. The cluster cannot be changed afterwards. FR-PROC-02, FR-PROC-03
  7. 7
    Already asked for. A card for each idea that has a requirement, with the tag "Asked for" or "Withdrawn". FR-PROC-02
  8. 8
    Your requirements. One card for each requirement. "None yet." with a sentence when there are none. FR-PROC-02
  9. 9
    An open requirement with no provider. Tags for the cluster, "Open" and the state. Then one sentence, the providers listed for it (name order, name and summary, no fee), the pool sentence (a lower bound in steps of five from five companies, nothing below), the choose form and the withdraw form. The choose button cannot be undone: the provider cannot be changed afterwards. Withdraw is drawn as a red button. FR-PROC-04, FR-PROC-05, FR-PROC-06, FR-PROC-11, FR-DEPL-01
  10. 10
    A requirement with a provider. "You chose <name>.", a button-shaped link to the delivery page, and a closed section holding the pool and the providers. No forms. FR-DEPL-01, FR-UX-24
  11. 11
    Your next step. The card at the foot, because the owner's turn is on another stage. FR-UX-18

Other states of this view

  • No signed-off assessment: "A requirement is made from an idea you accepted in an assessment you signed off. You have not signed one off yet." with a link "Go to your assessment".
  • No provider is listed for a requirement in the owner's cluster: "No provider is listed for this in your cluster yet. ..." and no choose form; the withdraw form remains.
  • The page shows only the newest signed-off assessment's ideas (FSD section 12, item 17).

SCR-06 Delivery

Lets the owner write and agree a milestone plan with the chosen provider, record what happened to each milestone in the fixed order, note a payment made outside the service, and close the engagement.

A delivery waits for the owner's decision

Drawn from: Observed in the capture (owner C); read in app/deliver/page.tsx and modules/deliver/src/summary.ts.

  1. 1
    The five steps. Deliver is the page and the owner's turn. FR-UX-17
  2. 2
    Head. "Step 4 of 5 · Deliver", the title "Delivery" and one sentence: "Write the plan, record each delivery, accept or reject it against your test, and note what you paid." FR-UX-16
  3. 3
    Where things stand. One tag (Your turn; Waiting for the provider; Nothing to do now; All closed) and one sentence, with a link to the first milestone that waits for a decision. FR-UX-23
  4. 4
    An engagement. One card for each: the requirement's title, tags for status (Planning, In progress, Complete, Ended early) and cluster, the provider, a bar with its words, and the date the plan was agreed. FR-DEPL-01, FR-DEPL-06
  5. 5
    Milestones. A numbered list. Once the plan is agreed no milestone can be added or changed. Each milestone shows a state tag (Not started, Delivered waiting for you, Accepted, Rejected, Paid), its date and price, how the owner will check it, any payment noted, and its history as dated lines. FR-DEPL-02, FR-DEPL-04
  6. 6
    The decision. Only the forms the state of a milestone allows are drawn. A delivered milestone has the decision form: a note and two buttons, the first the main button. The provider does not use this service, so "delivered" is the owner recording what the provider said. FR-DEPL-04, FR-DEPL-07
  7. 7
    Totals. "Plan total ..." and "Payments you have recorded: ...", in rupees with Indian grouping. A payment is one the owner made outside the service; nothing is compared with the price. FR-DEPL-05
  8. 8
    Close. Ends the engagement; everything stays on record. FR-DEPL-06
  9. 9
    About this page. The tag SYNTHETIC and what the page is: the owner's own record. It does not contact the provider, hold money or move any. The provider has not seen any of it. This is at the foot of the page, not under the title. FR-UX-01, FR-UX-24

Other states of this view

  • No engagement yet: "No engagement yet. Choose a provider for one of your requirements on What you need" and the card "About this page".
  • A milestone rejected after being delivered as many times as can be recorded: a notice, and no delivery form.
  • Complete or ended early: milestones with history and no forms.

Writing the plan, before it is agreed

Drawn from: Read in app/deliver/page.tsx. The capture has no engagement in this state.

  1. 1
    Add a milestone. Four fields: what is delivered, how the owner will check it (written now, before any work), a due date written like 2026-12-15, and a price in whole rupees. Up to 12 milestones. FR-DEPL-02
  2. 2
    Agree the plan. Drawn only once the plan has a milestone. Agreeing locks the plan, and nothing can be delivered, accepted or paid before then. FR-DEPL-03

SCR-08 · /operator · app/operator/page.tsx · FSD section 5.9

SCR-08 Providers and the listing fee

Lets the operator set the one listing fee, add providers, list and unlist them, and see the pools that have reached five companies.

The operator's page

Drawn from: Observed in the capture (the operator, three providers listed, one pool at the floor); read in app/operator/page.tsx.

  1. 1
    Head. The title and one sentence: "Add providers, list them so owners can choose, and set the one listing fee." There is no step line, no strip and no next step: the operator has no steps. FR-UX-16, FR-OPS-01
  2. 2
    The SYNTHETIC line. The tag and: every provider is a placeholder; no company is named or shown; no money is collected, a listing records the fee that is due and nothing more. FR-UX-01
  3. 3
    At a glance. Three figures side by side from 40 rem wide: the fee in force ("Not decided" before one is set), providers listed out of providers added, and the number of pools at the floor. The fee here is the placeholder written by the demonstration seed (SYNTHETIC). FR-PROC-07, FR-PROC-10
  4. 4
    Listing terms. One fee at a time, the same for every provider. A new version applies to providers listed from now on; those already listed keep the fee they were listed at, and the page says so when listed providers pay different fees.
