Owner: the deliver module. Paths: `modules/deliver/`, `app/deliver/`, `app/api/companies/[id]/engagements/`, `app/api/companies/[id]/requirements/[rid]/engagement/`, and migrations 0010 and 0011 in `drizzle/`. Last updated 5 October 2026.

This is slice 5, Deploy: after the owner has made a requirement (slice 4) they choose one of the providers listed for it, write a plan of milestones with the test they will check each one by and the price, agree the plan, and then record what happens: delivered (as the provider told them), accepted or rejected against their own test, a payment they made, and the close. Protected paths touched: data scope (a company's engagements, read and written only by that company's owner), migrations (three append-only tables and 23 guard triggers) and money (the owner's own record of a price and a payment). A separate agent has not yet read this slice; see [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md). No person has read it.

**No money moves here.** The platform does not hold, release, escrow or send any money, does not contact the provider, and does not charge a fee on any of it. A "payment recorded" is a number the owner types about a payment they made themselves. Providers do not sign in (OQ-13), so everything on a delivery is the owner's own record. Whether the platform will ever sit between owner and provider is OQ-12, undecided.

## What works

- **Choosing a provider.** On `/procure`, an open requirement with a listed provider offers a "choose a provider" form. The choice is recorded once per requirement. Choosing the same provider again returns the engagement already there (200, not 201); a different one is refused (`already_engaged`). The database refuses a requirement that is withdrawn, and a provider that is not listed now or does not serve that idea in that cluster. A provider unlisted later does not end an engagement already made. Once a provider is chosen the requirement can no longer be withdrawn (a trigger); the owner closes the engagement instead.
- **The plan.** Up to 12 milestones, each with a name (120 characters), the test the owner will check it by (500), a due date written YYYY-MM-DD that is a real date, and a price in whole rupees (1 to 100,000,000). Milestones are numbered in order by the statement that adds them. The owner agrees the plan once, only when it has at least one milestone, and after that no milestone can be added.
- **What happens next** is a sequence the database enforces and `src/state.ts` mirrors (a test reads the triggers and checks the two agree): nothing is delivered, accepted, rejected or paid before the plan is agreed; a milestone is delivered, then accepted or rejected; a rejected one can be delivered again, at most 14 times (an assumption); an accepted one is paid once; after the close nothing more is recorded. The state of a milestone and of the engagement (planning, in progress, complete, ended early) is worked out from the events on every read, never stored. An engagement is complete only if it was closed with every milestone accepted; otherwise it ended early.
- **The page** `/deliver` shows each engagement with its plan, each milestone's state and history, and only the forms that can work right now (the server decides, not the page). Every refusal of a form post goes back to the page with a message, never a page of JSON; another company's engagement gets the message a missing one gets. Money is shown with Indian digit grouping.
- **The scope rule** is the platform's own. Another company's owner, and the operator, get a 404 identical to a missing record, audited (`engagement.read.denied`, `engagement.write.denied`). A person in the company who is not its owner may read and gets 403 on any change. Every query is bound to the signed-in person's company; a milestone or an engagement named under another company is a 404 and writes nothing. A static test checks every SQL string on the three tables and that only the data layer touches them.
- **Append-only, in the database.** The three tables refuse UPDATE, DELETE and any INSERT that would replace a row, on every unique key (a platform test reads the keys from the migrated database and fails if a key has no trigger that looks at it). Fourteen further triggers enforce the order above, the limits and the references. Every write also writes an audit entry in the same batch, only when a row was added.
- **D-03.** Nothing here reads or writes a fee. The three tables hold what was chosen, planned and recorded and no fee, share or commission; the only money columns are `milestones.agreed_inr` and `engagement_events.amount_inr`, both the owner's own figures. A static test pins the column lists, and that no SQL statement reads those two together with the listing fee, the listing terms or the provider's listings, or adds them up across companies. A browser and a smoke test check that no page the owner sees contains the fee. An integration test lists two providers at different fees, chooses the dearer, runs the whole path to the close, and checks that the listing terms and the listings are byte-for-byte unchanged afterwards.

## Not built

- **Anything for the provider.** No sign-in, no view of the plan, no way to say "delivered" themselves, no notification. "Delivered" is what the owner records the provider told them.
- **Money.** No payment, escrow, invoice, refund, tax or receipt. The thesis says payment is tied to acceptance; here the record of a payment can only be made after acceptance, and nothing more is promised. A recorded amount may be more or less than the agreed price and nothing compares them except the two totals on the page.
- **Changing a plan after it is agreed**, splitting or merging milestones, partial acceptance, a dispute, a deadline passing, an extension. The due date is shown; nothing reads it.
- **Changing your mind about the provider.** One engagement per requirement and a requirement with one chosen cannot be withdrawn. Closing an engagement ends it; it does not reopen the requirement for another choice. Whether it should is OQ-15.
- **Outcomes** (did the thing do what the assessment said it would): slice 6.
- **Anything between an engagement and a pool.** Engagements are not pooled.
- English only; no export or print; no search; the page shows every engagement.

## Known limits

1. The acceptance test is a sentence the owner writes. Nothing checks that it is a test, that it is measurable, or that the owner applied it. "Accepted" means the owner pressed the button.
2. The owner is both the buyer and the only recorder. There is no second person and no check by anyone else, as in slice 3 (OQ-08).
3. Dates are text checked for being a real calendar date between 2000 and 2100. A due date in the past is allowed. Timestamps come from the Worker's clock.
4. The limits (12 milestones, 14 deliveries per milestone, 100,000,000 rupees, 120, 500 and 500 characters) are assumptions. Each is in the code and in the database, and a test keeps the two equal. After a milestone's last delivery is rejected, nothing more can be done with it but close the engagement.
5. A requirement with a provider chosen can never be withdrawn or given another provider (OQ-15). A wrong click is permanent: the owner can only close the engagement.
6. The provider's name and summary are those in the registry now. The registry is append-only and has no edit, so they do not change, but a provider unlisted later still shows under its engagement.
7. The guards test and the health check confirm that triggers exist by name. They do not run them; the integration tests prove behaviour with real SQL, one probe per rule, and a trigger with the right name and a weaker body would pass the name checks (the same limit as slice 3, limit 6).
8. Two requests racing for the same step are decided by SQLite serialising writers, one statement and the triggers. A test sends them one after the other, inside one process. Two Workers racing were not run.
9. This module reads the procure tables: the engagement list joins to the requirement (on id and company) and to the provider's name and summary, and the insert checks the requirement. The procure static test records this as an exception, with the shape it may take.
10. Denied attempts write audit rows without a cap, as everywhere (platform limit 8).
11. The page was run in Chromium only, against a local Worker, with a synthetic sign-in. The inherited platform limits apply.
