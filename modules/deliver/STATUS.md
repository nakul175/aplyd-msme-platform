# Deliver module: status

Owner: the deliver module. Paths: `modules/deliver/`, `app/deliver/`, `app/api/companies/[id]/engagements/`, `app/api/companies/[id]/requirements/[rid]/engagement/`, and migrations 0010 and 0011 in `drizzle/`. It also adds a "choose a provider" form to `app/procure/page.tsx`, a refusal to withdraw a requirement that has a provider chosen (`modules/procure`), and a link on the home page. Last updated 5 October 2026 (the page rebuilt, slice 10c).

This is slice 5, Deploy: after the owner has made a requirement (slice 4) they choose one of the providers listed for it, write a plan of milestones with the test they will check each one by and the price, agree the plan, and then record what happens: delivered (as the provider told them), accepted or rejected against their own test, a payment they made, and the close. Protected paths touched: data scope (a company's engagements, read and written only by that company's owner), migrations (three append-only tables and 23 guard triggers) and money (the owner's own record of a price and a payment). A separate agent has not yet read this slice; see [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md). No person has read it.

**No money moves here.** The platform does not hold, release, escrow or send any money, does not contact the provider, and does not charge a fee on any of it. A "payment recorded" is a number the owner types about a payment they made themselves. Providers do not sign in (OQ-13), so everything on a delivery is the owner's own record. Whether the platform will ever sit between owner and provider is OQ-12, undecided.

## What works

- **Choosing a provider.** On `/procure`, an open requirement with a listed provider offers a "choose a provider" form. The choice is recorded once per requirement. Choosing the same provider again returns the engagement already there (200, not 201); a different one is refused (`already_engaged`). The database refuses a requirement that is withdrawn, and a provider that is not listed now or does not serve that idea in that cluster. A provider unlisted later does not end an engagement already made. Once a provider is chosen the requirement can no longer be withdrawn (a trigger); the owner closes the engagement instead.
- **The plan.** Up to 12 milestones, each with a name (120 characters), the test the owner will check it by (500), a due date written YYYY-MM-DD that is a real date, and a price in whole rupees (1 to 100,000,000). Milestones are numbered in order by the statement that adds them. The owner agrees the plan once, only when it has at least one milestone, and after that no milestone can be added.
- **What happens next** is a sequence the database enforces and `src/state.ts` mirrors (a unit test reads the trigger text and compares it with the table in `state.ts`; an integration test runs the real triggers, rolled back, for every kind on seven milestones in different states and checks that the page is offered exactly what the database would accept): nothing is delivered, accepted, rejected or paid before the plan is agreed; a milestone is delivered, then accepted or rejected; a rejected one can be delivered again, at most 14 times (an assumption); an accepted one is paid once; after the close nothing more is recorded. The state of a milestone and of the engagement (planning, in progress, complete, ended early) is worked out from the events on every read, never stored. An engagement is complete only if it was closed with every milestone accepted; otherwise it ended early.
- **The page** `/deliver` shows each engagement with its plan, each milestone's state and history, and only the forms that can work right now (the server decides, not the page). A milestone rejected for the 14th time has no form left, and the page says why and that closing the engagement is the way out. Every refusal of a form post that happens once the person is known goes back to the page with a message, not a page of JSON; another company's engagement gets the message a missing one gets. An exception: a form post refused *before* the person is known (no session 401, another company named in the path 404, the operator 404, a request another website started 403, a body over 64 KB 413) is answered with JSON, because the shared platform check `authorizeCompanyWrite` does not know it is a form; see modules/run/STATUS.md and its test, which pins each answer. Money is shown with Indian digit grouping.
- **The scope rule** is the platform's own. Another company's owner, and the operator, get a 404 identical to a missing record, audited (`engagement.read.denied`, `engagement.write.denied`). A person in the company who is not its owner may read and gets 403 on any change. Every query is bound to the signed-in person's company; a milestone or an engagement named under another company is a 404 and writes nothing. A static test checks every SQL string on the three tables and that only the data layer touches them.
- **Append-only, in the database.** The three tables refuse UPDATE, DELETE and any INSERT that would replace a row, on every unique key (a platform test reads the keys from the migrated database and fails if a key has no trigger that looks at it). Fourteen further triggers enforce the order above, the limits and the references. Every write also writes an audit entry in the same batch, only when a row was added.
- **D-03.** Nothing here reads or writes a fee. The three tables hold what was chosen, planned and recorded and no fee, share or commission; the only money columns are `milestones.agreed_inr` and `engagement_events.amount_inr`, both the owner's own figures. A static test pins the column lists, and that no SQL statement reads those two together with the listing fee, the listing terms or the provider's listings, or adds them up across companies. A browser and a smoke test check that no page the owner sees contains the fee. An integration test lists two providers at different fees, chooses the dearer, runs the whole path to the close, and checks that the listing terms and the listings are byte-for-byte unchanged afterwards.

## The page after the interface rebuild (slice 10c)

`/deliver` ("Delivery") was rebuilt on the shared interface of slice 10 (`app/globals.css`, `app/_ui/`, [the interface guide](../../docs/design/INTERFACE-GUIDE.md)). Behaviour did not change: the same forms post the same fields to the same endpoints, the redirects and `?made=`, `?saved=` and `?problem=` keys are the same, and every message, label, button name and test id that a test finds the page by is the same. What changed is what a person sees.

- **The top of the page** shows the five steps (Deliver underlined), then one card on where things stand: a tag ("Your turn", "Waiting for the provider", "Nothing to do now" or "All closed") and one sentence ("1 milestone is waiting for your decision.", "Write your plan and agree it. Nothing can be delivered, accepted or paid before then.", and so on). When a delivery waits for the owner the card also has a link, "Go to milestone N", to that milestone (a plain in-page anchor; no script).
- **Each engagement is a card**: its title, its state as a tag (Planning, In progress, Complete, Ended early), its cluster, the provider, and a bar with "N of M milestones accepted" and the date the plan was agreed or the engagement closed. The plan is a column of **milestone cards**, each with its number and title, a state tag, the due date and the price (Indian digit grouping), "How you will check it" on its own line, the payment the owner noted, the history, and only the forms the server offers for that state. A short line joins one card to the next and turns the brand colour once the card above it is accepted. A milestone waiting for the owner's decision has a marigold edge. Under the cards, one line gives "Plan total" and "Payments you have recorded".
- **Writing the plan is two plain steps**, shown only until the plan is agreed: "Add a milestone" (each box with a help line), and "Agree the plan" once there is a milestone. After that the page says the plan is agreed and locked, as before.
- **One main button per engagement**, chosen by `primaryAction` in `src/summary.ts`: the owner's decision on a delivery first, then noting a payment, then noting that the provider says a part is done, the earliest milestone first. Every other form that is allowed stays on the page with a plain button. "Accept milestone N" is the main button when a delivery waits; "Reject milestone N" is a plain one.
- **Closing is the quiet option at the foot of the card**: "Done with this engagement?", the optional reason, a plain "Close this engagement" button and the sentence that closing ends it and everything stays on record.
- **The SYNTHETIC statement and the sentences that say the page records and moves no money** are in a visible "About this page" card at the foot of the page, in the same words, split into three paragraphs. They are not in a collapsed section: the browser test reads the text a person sees, and a collapsed section is not in it. The sentences next to the buttons that record a delivery and a payment say the same thing at the point of use.
- **Words that were added**, each restating what this page or the module already does and none checked by a test of its own: the form titles ("When the provider says this part is done", "Note down what you paid"), "Check it against the test above, then decide.", the help lines under the fields and buttons ("It starts as the agreed price. Change it to what you actually paid.", "The provider does not use this service, so you record what they told you. The milestone then waits for your decision.", "Accept it if it passes the test you wrote. Reject it if it does not: it can then be delivered again. Your answer is recorded and stays on record.", "It joins the plan above. You can add more before you agree the plan.", "Closing ends the engagement: nothing more can be recorded on it. Everything stays on record."), and the sentence about the maximum price, which moved from the label ("Price for this milestone, in rupees (up to ₹10,00,00,000)") to the help line under it so that the two fields of a row line up; the tests find the field by `/^Price for this milestone/`.
- **Logic that was added** is in `src/summary.ts` and tested in `tests/unit/summary.test.ts` (8 tests): how many milestones are accepted and the words under the bar, the state of the whole page in one sentence, the main button, and the first delivery waiting. Nothing in it reads a fee or another company's record.
- **No script runs on the page.** It is server-rendered with plain forms, and has its own small stylesheet (`app/deliver/deliver.css`, every class prefixed `dl-`).

## Not built

- **Anything for the provider.** No sign-in, no view of the plan, no way to say "delivered" themselves, no notification. "Delivered" is what the owner records the provider told them.
- **Money.** No payment, escrow, invoice, refund, tax or receipt. The thesis says payment is tied to acceptance; here the record of a payment can only be made after acceptance, and nothing more is promised. A recorded amount may be more or less than the agreed price and nothing compares them except the two totals on the page.
- **Changing a plan after it is agreed**, splitting or merging milestones, partial acceptance, a dispute, a deadline passing, an extension. The due date is shown; nothing reads it.
- **Changing your mind about the provider.** One engagement per requirement and a requirement with one chosen cannot be withdrawn. Closing an engagement ends it; it does not reopen the requirement for another choice. Whether it should is OQ-15.
- **Outcomes** (did the thing do what the assessment said it would): slice 6.
- **Anything between an engagement and a pool.** Engagements are not pooled.
- English only; no export or print; no search; the page shows every engagement.

## Known limits

1. The acceptance test is a sentence the owner writes. Nothing checks that it is a test, that it is measurable, or that the owner applied it. "Accepted" means the owner pressed the button.
2. The owner is both the buyer and the only recorder. There is no second person and no check by anyone else, as in slice 3 (OQ-08).
3. Dates are text checked for being a real calendar date between 2000 and 2100. A due date in the past is allowed. Timestamps come from the Worker's clock.
4. The limits (12 milestones, 14 deliveries per milestone, 100,000,000 rupees, 120, 500 and 500 characters) are assumptions. Each is in the code and in the database, and a test keeps the two equal. After a milestone's 14th delivery is rejected, nothing more can be done with it but close the engagement; the page says so and offers no delivery form (the first version offered one the database refused: found by the independent review).
5. A requirement with a provider chosen can never be withdrawn or given another provider (OQ-15). A wrong click is permanent: the owner can only close the engagement.
6. The provider's name and summary are those in the registry now. The registry is append-only and has no edit, so they do not change, but a provider unlisted later still shows under its engagement.
7. The guards test and the health check confirm that triggers exist by name. They do not run them; the integration tests prove behaviour with real SQL, one probe per rule, and a trigger with the right name and a weaker body would pass the name checks (the same limit as slice 3, limit 6).
8. Two requests racing for the same step are decided by SQLite serialising writers, one statement and the triggers. A test sends them one after the other, inside one process. Two Workers racing were not run.
9. This module reads the procure tables: the engagement list joins to the requirement (on id and company) and to the provider's name and summary, and the insert checks the requirement. The procure static test records this as an exception, with the shape it may take.
10. Denied attempts write audit rows without a cap, as everywhere (platform limit 8).
11. The page was run in Chromium only, against a local Worker, with a synthetic sign-in. The inherited platform limits apply.
12. **A closed engagement's requirement still counts in the demand pool.** The pool counts a requirement as live unless it was withdrawn, and a requirement with an engagement cannot be withdrawn, so a company that chose a provider and then closed the engagement is still counted towards the five-company threshold for that idea and cluster. The effect is stale or inflated demand, not a leak (the threshold is still applied to a company count). Part of OQ-15; not changed here.
13. **A payment is not compared with the agreed price.** A payment of ₹99,999,999 against a ₹40,000 milestone is accepted; "over the limit" means only the 100,000,000 ceiling. Deliberate (the amount is the owner's own record), and the page shows both totals.
14. **The page and the API differ on who may view.** `/deliver` shows "not linked to a company" to anyone who is not an owner, while the API lets a person in the company read. A consistency point, not a leak.
15. **Reads are not bounded across a company.** The list loads every milestone and event of the company and filters in the application, and the number of engagements per company is not capped (one per requirement, and requirements are bounded by the assessment). A single read is bound to its engagement (fixed after the review; a unit test checks the statements). A milestone holds at most 29 events and a plan 12 milestones.
16. **Two triggers could answer the same refusal; they no longer do (migration 0014, review-fixes branch).** A 15th delivery on a milestone whose 14th was awaiting a decision matched both the "delivered once, or after a rejection" rule and the delivery cap, and an engagement for a withdrawn requirement with an unlisted provider matched two rules. Both triggers are recreated so that each applies to one case, and a test names the one message for each. The rules added in the same migration (limit 17) can overlap other rules for a row that breaks several things at once (a payment with no amount on a milestone that is not accepted, say): the row is refused either way and the message is then not defined. Each new rule is tested on a row that breaks nothing else.
17. **The tables now hold what the handlers check (migration 0014, review-fixes branch), with three gaps.** Found by the review, by direct SQL: a fractional `amount_inr` (1.5) or `agreed_inr` (5000.5), a `due_on` that is not a real date (`9999-99-99`), a title of only spaces, a payment with no amount, and an event note of any length were accepted, because the checks were on range and shape (a CHECK that comes out NULL passes, and an integer column keeps 1.5). Triggers now refuse each: a whole number for `seq`, `agreed_inr` and `amount_inr`, a payment has an amount, a due date is a real date between 2000 and 2100 (SQLite's `date()` turns 2026-02-30 into 2026-03-02, so a text that does not come back as itself is not a date), a name and a test are not only ASCII blanks, a note is at most 500 characters and 2,000 bytes (SQLite's `length()` stops counting at a NUL character, so the note triggers also bound the note at 2,000 bytes (the most 500 characters take in UTF-8); a short note that has a NUL in it is still stored, (found by the review of the review fixes).). **Gaps:** blank means ASCII blanks (a name of only non-breaking spaces is refused by the handler, which trims Unicode white space, and accepted by the table); a note's length is in characters; sizes of JSON are not checked by the table; a milestone's title and test are bounded by older CHECKs that count with `length()`, so text after a NUL is not counted there (the notes are bounded by the new triggers, in bytes too). **Overlaps still present:** the new rules refuse a row either way, but two rules can refuse the same row and which message answers is not defined: a milestone with a fractional price that is also out of range, and a second engagement for a requirement that already has one with a provider that is not listed (`engagements_no_replace` and the listed-provider rule: read in the SQL by the review, not run). Until 0014 is applied (it is on the review-fixes branch, not on slice 5's), the database holds none of this and a second writer could record "₹1.50 paid".

## Slice 11b (6 October 2026): the warning beside Accept, and help to write a test someone else could apply

Built by an agent at the sponsor's instruction; no person has read it.

- **A warning beside "Accept milestone N" (FND-183).** While an engagement is open, nothing on it is accepted and no starting number is recorded, the decision form says: "You have not recorded your starting number. Once you accept a milestone, it can no longer be recorded." with the link "Record the starting number first" to the place on Results (`/run#bl-<engagement>`). Once a number is recorded, or anything is accepted, or the engagement is closed, the sentence is not drawn. The rule is `acceptEndsBaseline` in `src/summary.ts` (unit-tested); the page reads which engagements have a starting number with the run module's `listOutcomes` (one more read for each engagement on the page). The journey's home-page rule `record_baseline` already asks for the number first while a delivery waits; this says it at the button.
- **What a good test looks like (BR-DEPL-08).** The add-milestone form's help under "How you will check it" says to write the test so that someone else could check it (what is measured, the number that counts as done and its unit, who checks it, and by when), and a collapsed "What a good test looks like" shows one worked example, tagged SYNTHETIC, split into those five parts. The example's figures are made up to show the parts; they are not a figure for any factory.
- **A vague test is flagged, not blocked.** `testQuality` in `src/test-quality.ts` names what a test lacks: a number (digits or a number word) and a unit or counted thing (a word from a list, or `%`, `₹`, `°`). A saved milestone whose test lacks either carries one sentence under "How you will check it": "This test has no number [and no unit], so someone else could not tell whether it was met. When you decide on this milestone, write in your note what you counted or measured." Saving is never refused for it. The word lists are assumptions written for this check, not a standard, and the check reads words, not meaning: a test with a number and a unit is not thereby a good one, and a sensible test can be flagged.
- **An empty test is still refused**, as before (the handler, and the CHECK `milestones_test` and trigger `milestones_text_is_not_blank` of applied migrations). The BRD row says an empty test is "flagged, not blocked"; this slice keeps the refusal, because the database requires the text and applied migrations are not changed. `testQuality("")` returns `empty` for a caller that wants it.
- **A milestone cannot be edited**, so a flagged test cannot be rewritten once saved; the sentence says what the owner can do instead (write what was measured in the decision note). The flag is shown on the page only, not in the JSON of API-17 and API-18.

## Slice 12d (6 October 2026): no Accept before the starting number, one "your turn" card, forms that keep what was typed

Built by an agent at the sponsor's instruction (wave 2); no person has read it. The database rules, the JSON interface and the migrations were not changed.

- **No Accept while the starting number can still be lost (S5-03).** While an engagement is open, nothing on it is accepted and no starting number is recorded (`acceptEndsBaseline`), the decision form draws no "Accept milestone N" button. Its notice says why and links "Write down your starting number first" to `/run#bl-<engagement>`. "Reject milestone N" stays, because rejecting loses nothing. Once a number is recorded the button is drawn as before. The database still allows an acceptance without a starting number (JSON, or a stale form opened before this change); only the page stops offering it. - **One "your turn" card at the top (S5-06, S5-17).** The shared frame (`app/_ui/parts.tsx`) draws the owner's turn from the journey's `nextStep` directly under the page head when the page passes in-page places for it (`here`). When the card is drawn, the page's own summary card is not (the card says the same). When the owner's turn is on another page, the card stays at the foot and the summary says "Nothing on this page needs your decision right now. - **Plain words (S5-05, S5-08).** The lede says "APLYD does not put you in touch with the provider. You will have to contact them yourself." The eyebrow reads "Step 4 of 5: Deliver". Dates on the page read "15 December 2026" (`formatDate`).
