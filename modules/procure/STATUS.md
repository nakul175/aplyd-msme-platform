# Procure module: status

Owner: the procure module. Paths: `modules/procure/`, `app/procure/`, `app/operator/`, `app/api/providers/`, `app/api/operator/`, `app/api/companies/[id]/requirements/`, and migrations 0008 and 0009 in `drizzle/`. It also adds the operator check to `modules/platform/src/api/authorize.ts`. Last updated 5 October 2026.

This is slice 4: from an idea the owner accepted in a signed-off assessment (slice 3), the owner makes a standard requirement in a cluster; requirements of the same kind in the same cluster are counted as a pool; a platform operator keeps a registry of providers and lists them at one flat fee; the owner sees the providers listed for what they asked for. Protected paths touched: data scope (a company's requirements, read and written only by that company's owner, and the first query that reads across companies), migrations (six append-only tables and 23 guard triggers) and money (the listing fee, the first money in the system). No agent other than the one that wrote it has read this slice yet, and no person has; see [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md).

**No fee is decided.** The listing fee is one number the operator sets, the same for every provider (D-03). Until the operator sets one, nothing can be listed. Values in tests and on the synthetic pages are placeholders and not a proposal (OQ-11). Nothing charges the fee: there is no invoice, billing or payment here.

## What works

- **A requirement.** On `/procure` the owner sees the ideas they accepted in the latest assessment they signed off, and for each chooses a cluster (three SYNTHETIC ones; the first real sector and cluster are OQ-03) and makes a requirement. It keeps a copy of what the assessment said about the idea (title, summary, AI or not, costs, source, library version) so a later change to the library changes nothing already made. One requirement per company, assessment and idea; asking again returns the one already there (200, not 201), even for another cluster. The database refuses a requirement from an assessment that is not signed off, from an idea that was not accepted (the latest decision counts), or from another company's assessment.
- **Withdrawing.** A requirement is withdrawn once, with an optional note. It stays on record, marked withdrawn, and leaves its pool and its provider list.
- **Pools and the floor of five (C-04).** A pool is the open requirements of one idea in one cluster; it counts companies, not requirements, so a company counts once. Below five companies the number is shown to nobody: not to the owners in it, and not to the operator, whose list holds only pools of five or more, as counts with no company, user or requirement id. At five or more, every member sees the number. This is the cautious reading of C-04 and is OQ-14. Two queries read requirements across companies; a static test requires them to select counts of distinct companies and nothing else, and every other read of a company's requirements to be bound to that company.
- **The provider registry.** The operator (a user with the operator role and no company) adds a provider with a name, a short summary and what it serves: ideas from the opportunity library, each in listed clusters. The registry is append-only: nothing is edited or deleted, and a provider that serves nothing cannot be listed.
- **One flat listing fee (D-03).** The operator sets the fee in whole rupees; a new fee is a new version, earlier listings keep the version they were made under, and a stale version cannot be used. A listing copies the fee in force from the terms in the same statement (a caller cannot supply one, and the endpoint ignores a fee in the request), so every provider is listed at the fee in force when it was listed. Listing and unlisting alternate; the history stays. A static test pins the money columns: the only ones are `listing_terms.fee_inr` and its copy on a listing, and nothing in the schema refers a fee to a requirement, a company, an owner's choice or a pool.
- **What an owner sees of providers.** For each open requirement, the providers listed now that serve that idea in that cluster, in name order (case-insensitive, then id) and nothing else: not the fee, not the listing time, not demand. A provider never listed, or unlisted since, is shown to owners nowhere; only the operator's registry shows it. A static test requires every query that orders providers to order by name alone.
- **The scope rule** is the platform's own. Another company's owner, and the operator, get a 404 identical to a missing record, audited. A person in the company who is not its owner may read and gets 403 on any change. The operator endpoints refuse an owner, an account with the operator role that belongs to a company, an account with no company and no operator role, and a visitor, and audit the refusal.
- **Form posts** that cannot be done go back to the page (`/procure` or `/operator`) with a message, never a page of JSON; another company's record gets the message a missing one gets.
- **Append-only, in the database.** Six tables (requirements and their events, providers, their services and events, the listing terms) refuse UPDATE, DELETE and any INSERT that would replace a row on any unique key. Five further triggers refuse a requirement from an unsigned assessment or an unaccepted idea, a listing that does not carry the fee in force or has nothing to serve, and a listing that does not alternate with unlisting. Every write also writes an audit entry in the same batch, only when a row was added.

## Not built

- **Anything for the provider.** Providers do not sign in (OQ-13); the operator enters them. No quote, price from a provider, request for a quote, profile, rating, review or contact: the owner is shown names and summaries.
- **Acting on a pool.** A pool is a count. Nothing groups the requirements into a joint request, approaches a provider on their behalf or shares anything between companies.
- **Matching or ranking providers.** There is none and there must not be one that depends on fees (D-03). The list is in name order.
- **Charging the fee.** No invoice, billing, tax or payment; the fee is a number on a listing.
- **Choosing a provider** is slice 5 (the deliver module), which is stacked on this one and not part of this pull request.
- A real cluster list, real providers, any real fee: OQ-03, OQ-11. The three clusters are invented.
- English only; no search or paging of providers.

## Known limits

1. The operator is a role in the synthetic seed. Operator endpoints are the first that are not scoped to a company: the check is the role plus no company, in the platform's shared code, with a test for each way of failing it. There is no second operator, no separation of duties, and no review of what the operator enters (OQ-08).
2. The floor is checked on the count of companies in a pool. A member can still learn something by differencing: when their own requirement takes a pool from "not shown" to "shown", they know it now has at least five, and if they then withdraw and it drops back they know it had exactly five. The floor stops a number below five being published; it does not make a pool's size secret near the boundary.
3. The limits (provider name 100 characters, summary 300, note 500, at most 20 services per provider, fee at most 10,000,000 rupees) are assumptions. Nothing limits the number of providers the operator adds.
4. A provider's name is not unique; the operator can add two with the same name. Order is name then id.
5. The listing fee is one number for all providers by construction, but "one number" is a rule in this module's code and migrations. A new table or column elsewhere could add a revenue line that depends on the provider chosen; the static money-column test would fail it (any new column with a money word in its name must be named in the test), but a column with another name would not be seen.
6. The guards test and the health check confirm that triggers exist by name. They do not run them; the integration tests prove behaviour with real SQL, one probe per rule (the same limit as slice 3, limit 6).
7. Denied attempts write audit rows without a cap, as everywhere (platform limit 8).
8. The page was run in Chromium only, against a local Worker, with a synthetic sign-in. The inherited platform limits apply.
