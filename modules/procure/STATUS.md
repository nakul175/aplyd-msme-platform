# Procure module: status

Owner: the procure module. Paths: `modules/procure/`, `app/procure/`, `app/operator/`, `app/api/providers/`, `app/api/operator/`, `app/api/companies/[id]/requirements/`, and migrations 0008 and 0009 in `drizzle/`. It also adds the operator check to `modules/platform/src/api/authorize.ts`. Last updated 5 October 2026 (the two pages rebuilt, slice 10b).

This is slice 4: from an idea the owner accepted in a signed-off assessment (slice 3), the owner makes a standard requirement in a cluster; requirements of the same kind in the same cluster are counted as a pool; a platform operator keeps a registry of providers and lists them at one fee at a time; the owner sees the providers listed for what they asked for. Protected paths touched: data scope (a company's requirements, read and written only by that company's owner, and the first query that reads across companies), migrations (six append-only tables and 23 guard triggers) and money (the listing fee, the first money in the system). A separate agent has read this slice and its findings are answered in [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md); no person has read it.

**No fee is decided.** The listing fee is one number the operator sets, the same for every provider listed under it (D-03). Until the operator sets one, nothing can be listed. Values in tests and on the synthetic pages are placeholders and not a proposal (OQ-11). Nothing charges the fee: there is no invoice, billing or payment here.

## What works

- **A requirement.** On `/procure` the owner sees the ideas they accepted in the latest assessment they signed off, and for each chooses a cluster (three SYNTHETIC ones; the first real sector and cluster are OQ-03) and makes a requirement. It keeps a copy of what the assessment said about the idea (title, summary, AI or not, costs, source, library version) so a later change to the library changes nothing already made. One requirement per company, assessment and idea; asking again returns the one already there (200, not 201) with the cluster it was made in, even when another cluster was asked for, and a form says it was already made. A requirement that was withdrawn is not made again by asking (409 `previously_withdrawn`): to ask again the owner makes a new assessment (see limit 9). The database refuses a requirement from an assessment that is not signed off, from an idea that was not accepted (the latest decision counts), or from another company's assessment.
- **Withdrawing.** A requirement is withdrawn once, with an optional note. It stays on record, marked withdrawn, and leaves its pool and its provider list.
- **Pools and the floor of five (C-04).** A pool is the open requirements of one idea in one cluster; it counts companies, not requirements, so a company counts once. Below five companies the number is shown to nobody: not to the owners in it, and not to the operator, whose list holds only pools of five or more, as counts with no company, user or requirement id. At five or more, every member sees "at least 5", "at least 10" and so on, not the count. This is the cautious reading of C-04 and is OQ-14. Two queries read requirements across companies; a static test requires them to select counts of distinct companies and nothing else, and every other read of a company's requirements to be bound to that company.
- **The provider registry.** The operator (a user with the operator role and no company) adds a provider with a name, a short summary and what it serves: ideas from the opportunity library, each in listed clusters. The registry is append-only: nothing is edited or deleted, and a provider that serves nothing cannot be listed.
- **One flat listing fee (D-03).** The operator sets the fee in whole rupees; a new fee is a new version, earlier listings keep the version they were made under, and a stale version cannot be used. A listing copies the fee in force from the terms in the same statement (a caller cannot supply one, and the endpoint ignores a fee in the request), so every provider is listed at the fee in force when it was listed. Listing and unlisting alternate; the history stays. A static test pins the money columns: the only ones are `listing_terms.fee_inr` and its copy on a listing, and nothing in the schema refers a fee to a requirement, a company, an owner's choice or a pool.
- **What an owner sees of providers.** For each open requirement, the providers listed now that serve that idea in that cluster, in name order (case-insensitive, then id) and nothing else: not the fee, not the listing time, not demand. A provider never listed, or unlisted since, is shown to owners nowhere; only the operator's registry shows it. A static test requires every query that orders providers to order by name alone.
- **The scope rule** is the platform's own. Another company's owner, and the operator, get a 404 identical to a missing record, audited. A person in the company who is not its owner may read and gets 403 on any change. The operator endpoints refuse an owner, an account with the operator role that belongs to a company, an account with no company and no operator role, and a visitor, and audit the refusal.
- **Form posts** that cannot be done go back to the page (`/procure` or `/operator`) with a message once the person is known; another company's record gets the message a missing one gets. An exception: a form post refused *before* the person is known (no session 401, another company named in the path 404, the operator 404, a request another website started 403, a body over 64 KB 413) is answered with JSON, because the shared platform check `authorizeCompanyWrite` does not know it is a form; see modules/run/STATUS.md and its test, which pins each answer.
- **Append-only, in the database.** Six tables (requirements and their events, providers, their services and events, the listing terms) refuse UPDATE, DELETE and any INSERT that would replace a row on any unique key. Five further triggers refuse a requirement from an unsigned assessment or an unaccepted idea, a listing that does not carry the fee in force or has nothing to serve, and a listing that does not alternate with unlisting. Every write also writes an audit entry in the same batch, only when a row was added.

## The two pages after the interface rebuild (slice 10b)

`/procure` ("What you need") and `/operator` ("Providers and the listing fee") were rebuilt on the shared interface of slice 10 (`app/globals.css`, `app/_ui/`, [the interface guide](../../docs/design/INTERFACE-GUIDE.md)). Behaviour did not change: the same forms post the same fields to the same endpoints, the redirects and `?problem=` keys are the same, and every message, label, button name and test id that a test finds the pages by is the same. What changed is what a person sees.

- **The owner's page** opens with the five steps (this page's step underlined), one sentence on where things stand, tags for what is the owner's turn, what waits for somebody else and what is done, and a bar of "N of M accepted ideas asked for". Accepted ideas that have no requirement yet come first, marked "Ready to ask for", each with its cluster question and one button; the ones already asked for follow, as one line each. Each requirement is a card with its cluster and a status tag ("No provider chosen yet", "Provider chosen", "Withdrawn"), one sentence saying what the owner's next action is (or why there is none), the providers listed for it, the choose form (one primary button) and, quietly below, the pool note and the withdraw form. A requirement with a provider chosen shows who was chosen and a button to the delivery page; its pool and provider list are in a collapsed section.
- **The pool note** is drawn as a calm, neutral block (a lock when the number is withheld, an eye when a lower bound is shown), with the same words as before. The page says once, above the cluster questions, that a pool's size is shown only from five companies. It still prints only the lower bound (`At least ${r.pool.atLeast} companies`) and never reads an exact count; the static test that pins this passes.
- **Two sentences were added next to buttons**, from facts already in the code and the open questions, because the interface guide asks a page to say whether what it records can be changed: next to "Choose a provider for ..." that the provider cannot be changed for that requirement afterwards (OQ-15, [the deliver module's limit 5](../deliver/STATUS.md)), and next to "Make a requirement from ..." that the requirement can be withdrawn until a provider is chosen but its cluster cannot be changed (limit 9 above). One sentence next to a requirement with a provider chosen was shortened and its in-text link became a button; it names the delivery page instead of "there".
- **The operator's page** opens with a summary row (the fee in force or "Not decided", providers listed of providers added, pools at the floor), then Listing terms (the fee state as a tag, the placeholder status and the D-03 rule stated, the form), Providers (a table; one block per provider on a phone, with a Listed or Not listed tag, the fee each was listed at, and a List or Unlist button), Add a provider (the idea and cluster checkboxes in two columns) and Pools at the floor. The List and Unlist buttons show one word and carry the provider's name in their accessible name (`aria-label="List Alpha Works"`), which is what the tests and screen readers find them by. What a provider serves is grouped by idea ("Idea (Cluster 1, Cluster 2)"). The operator is still shown no company's record, and the sentence that says so is kept.
- **Logic that was added** is in `src/view.ts` and tested in `tests/unit/view.test.ts`: the state of a requirement, the counts, the sentence and the tags at the top of the owner's page, the operator's summary, the number of different fees, and the grouping of what a provider serves. Nothing in it reads a pool's size, a fee or another company's record.
- **No script runs on either page.** Both are server-rendered with plain forms. Each has its own small stylesheet (`app/procure/procure.css`, `app/operator/operator.css`, every class prefixed `pr-` or `op-`).

## Not built

- **Anything for the provider.** Providers do not sign in (OQ-13); the operator enters them. No quote, price from a provider, request for a quote, profile, rating, review or contact: the owner is shown names and summaries.
- **Acting on a pool.** A pool is a count. Nothing groups the requirements into a joint request, approaches a provider on their behalf or shares anything between companies.
- **Matching or ranking providers.** There is none and there must not be one that depends on fees (D-03). The list is in name order.
- **Charging the fee.** No invoice, billing, tax or payment; the fee is a number on a listing.
- **Choosing a provider** is slice 5 (the deliver module), which is stacked on this one and not part of this pull request.
- A real cluster list, real providers, any real fee: OQ-03, OQ-11. The three clusters are invented.
- English only; no search or paging of providers.

## Known limits

1. The operator is a role in the synthetic seed. Operator endpoints are the first that are not scoped to a company: the check is the role plus no company, in the platform's shared code, with a test for each way of failing it. There is no second operator, no separation of duties, and no review of what the operator enters (OQ-08).
2. The floor is checked on the count of companies in a pool, and from the floor up a member is shown a lower bound in steps of five ("at least 5", "at least 10"), not the count. The independent review of slice 4 showed that the exact count told a fifth company that the pool it joined had held exactly four. A member can still learn something by differencing: a company whose own requirement takes a pool from "not shown" to "shown" learns that the pool held at least four others, and if it then withdraws and the pool drops back it learns that the pool had been between five and nine. The floor stops a number below five being published; it does not make a pool's size secret at the boundary. The operator's list shows the exact count for pools of five or more.
3. The limits (provider name 100 characters, summary 300, note 500 (and at most 2,000 bytes in the table after migration 0014, because SQLite's `length()` stops counting at a NUL), at most 20 services per provider, fee at most 10,000,000 rupees) are assumptions. Nothing limits the number of providers the operator adds.
4. A provider's name is not unique; the operator can add two with the same name. Order is name then id.
5. The listing fee is one number for all providers by construction, but "one number" is a rule in this module's code and migrations. A new table or column elsewhere could add a revenue line that depends on the provider chosen; the static money-column test would fail it (any new column with a money word in its name must be named in the test), but a column with another name would not be seen.
6. The guards test and the health check confirm that triggers exist by name. They do not run them; the integration tests prove behaviour with real SQL, one probe per rule (the same limit as slice 3, limit 6).
7. Denied attempts write audit rows without a cap, as everywhere (platform limit 8).
8. The page was run in Chromium only, against a local Worker, with a synthetic sign-in. The inherited platform limits apply.
9. A withdrawn requirement cannot be made again, because there is one requirement per company, assessment and idea and the withdrawal is a separate append-only row. An owner who chose the wrong cluster and withdrew must make a new assessment (which needs a new version of the description) to ask again. Letting a withdrawn requirement be remade is a change to the key and to the migration; it is not built (found by the independent review of slice 4).
10. "One fee at a time" is not "one fee for every provider ever listed". A provider keeps the fee it was listed at when the operator changes the fee, so providers listed at different times can pay different fees; the operator page says so when it happens. Nothing ties a fee to which provider an owner chooses, so D-03 as written (earnings do not vary with the owner's choice) is not broken, but an operator could list a favoured provider at a low fee just before raising it. Whether fee changes should be allowed while providers are listed is a decision for the sponsor (OQ-11); a rule that forbids it would be a new trigger.
11. What a provider serves is fixed from its first listing on, listed or not (migration 0014, review-fixes branch): a trigger refuses a direct insert into `provider_services` for a provider that has any listing event, and a test covers a listed one, an unlisted one and one never listed (the registry may still be written to directly for a provider never listed; the application never does). Until 0014 is applied (it is not on slice 4's branch) a service can be added to a listed provider by writing to the table directly. The same migration holds a fee to a whole number of rupees (a check on the table said only "greater than zero", which 0.5 satisfies) and a note to 500 characters.
12. A visitor's refused request (401) is not audited and a signed-in owner's refused request (403) is; the audit entry carries text the requester chose, capped at 64 characters (the platform's limit 8 covers the log's growth).
13. If the operator changes the fee between a listing and the reply to it, the stored listing is right and the reply now names the stored fee too (the review found it named the newer one); the two cannot be interleaved in the sequential tests, so this is checked by reading the row, not by a race.
