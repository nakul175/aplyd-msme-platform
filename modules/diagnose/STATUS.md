# Diagnose module: status

Owner: the diagnose module. Paths: `modules/diagnose/`, `app/diagnose/`, `app/api/companies/[id]/assessments/`, and migrations 0005, 0006 and 0007 in `drizzle/`. Last updated 5 October 2026.

This is slice 3, Diagnose v0: from the owner's confirmed description of the factory, a ranked list of things to do about the problems they named, each labelled AI or not, with a benefit range, cost (one-time, every year, hidden), what to do first and the risk. The owner decides on each idea and signs the assessment off. Protected paths touched: data scope (a company's assessments, read and written only by that company's owner) and migrations (two append-only tables and thirteen guard triggers). A separate agent read the first version of this slice (commit a644577) and found one high, three medium and six low findings; the fixes are migration 0007 and the code and tests after it ([VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md)). No person has read this slice.

## What works

- An owner presses "Make an assessment from version N" on `/diagnose`. The engine reads the newest version of the description they confirmed (never a later draft), ranks the opportunity library against it, and the result is stored. Making it again from the same version, library and engine returns the assessment already there (200, not 201), because the engine is deterministic. A newly confirmed version gives a new assessment; the old one is untouched.
- The engine is rules, not a model. The ranking is written out in `src/engine.ts` and on the page ("How the ideas were ranked"): 10 points for each problem the owner named that the idea addresses, plus points for readiness (4, 2 or 0), fit with the yearly budget (2, 0 or -2), minus risk (0, 1 or 2) and 1 for a missing answer about the gap. There is no term for AI. A test turns every item's kind around and checks that no score or position moves.
- Each idea carries: AI or not, why it is there (in the owner's own answers), benefit range with its measure, one-time, yearly and first-year cost in rupees, hidden costs, what to do first or check, risk and its note, and the source. Ideas whose gap the owner's answers show does not exist (they already keep accounts in software, say) are listed as left out, with the answer that excluded them. The first eight are shown.
- A result stores a copy of every library fact it used, so changing the library never changes a past assessment.
- The owner decides on each idea (accept, later, no), with a note. The latest decision on an idea stands; earlier ones stay in the history. The sign-off needs a decision on every idea, and after it nothing can be decided. It stores who, when, and a SHA-256 fingerprint of the assessment's id and result and the decision standing on each idea, with its note. Every read recomputes the fingerprint and says whether it still matches. The fingerprint does not cover who made a decision or when, the sign-off's own note, or the earlier decisions that a later one replaced (limit 3).
- The scope rule is the platform's own. Another company's owner, and the operator, get a 404 identical to a missing record, and the attempt is audited (`assessment.read.denied`, `assessment.write.denied`). A person in the company who is not its owner may read, and gets 403 on any change. An assessment id from another company, named under one's own company, is a 404. Every query is bound to the signed-in person's company; a static test checks every SQL string on both tables and that only the data layer touches them.
- The database, not only the application, holds the rules. Both tables refuse UPDATE, DELETE and any INSERT that would replace a row: an assessment on its id or on its inputs, an event on its id. A second sign-off for the same assessment is refused by a trigger of its own, so `INSERT OR REPLACE` cannot delete the first (the review's high finding; before migration 0007 it could). Further triggers refuse an assessment from a draft or missing version, a decision on an idea that is not in the assessment, a decision after sign-off, a sign-off with an undecided idea, a sign-off on an assessment with no ideas, and a 201st decision on one assessment (200 is an assumption). A composite foreign key stops an event from naming another company's assessment. The health check reports `degraded` if any guard is missing. A test in the platform reads every unique key of every append-only table from the migrated database and requires a BEFORE INSERT trigger that looks at each one, so a key added later without a matching trigger fails.
- Created decisions and sign-offs write an audit entry in the same batch, and only when the row was added. The sign-off statement carries the number of decisions its fingerprint was computed over; if another landed meanwhile it changes nothing and says "changed".
- A form post that cannot be done (an assessment that is not there, a decision that landed while the owner was signing off, a full assessment, an empty one) goes back to the page with a message, not to a page of JSON. Another company's assessment gets exactly the message a missing one gets.
- The library, the ranking points and the engine's output for eight fixed descriptions are pinned by hash to LIBRARY_VERSION and ENGINE_VERSION. Changing any of them without a new version, or recording a version without its pin, fails a test.
- The page shows all of this in rupees with Indian digit grouping, says in the page what drafted it ("by a set of rules, not by an AI model"), and marks every figure SYNTHETIC.

## Not built

- The library is SYNTHETIC. Seventeen entries (13 without AI, 4 with) whose titles, conditions, figures and risks are placeholders. None is sourced; every figure is marked as an assumption. They exist to exercise the engine and must not be shown to a real owner as advice. Real content needs OQ-09 (the right to reuse the study's material, with disclosure), OQ-03 (the first sector) and a named custodian (OQ-04).
- No sector-specific content. The questionnaire's sector answer is carried in the result but nothing in the library or the ranking depends on it.
- No advisor or consultant. The thesis says "AI drafts; a person checks"; here the person who checks is the owner, because adding a second person who reads a company's record is a data-scope change that needs its own review and decisions about who that person is (OQ-08). Slice 3 deliberately does not widen the scope rule.
- No AI. Nothing calls a model. If a model ever drafts items or explanations, that is a new slice with its own evidence.
- Nothing about what happens after "accept". An accepted idea does not yet become a requirement; that is slice 4.
- Re-assessing after a change to a draft. Only confirmed versions are used, so a draft changes nothing until the owner confirms it.
- English only; no export; no print view; no comparison between two assessments.

## Known limits

1. The ranking weights (10, 4, 2, 2, -2, risk, -1) are choices, not findings. They produce a defensible order for the synthetic library; nothing shows they are the right trade-offs for a real owner.
2. Costs are compared with the top of the yearly budget band the questionnaire offers (round numbers, assumptions). The first year counts the one-time cost and one year of the recurring cost; benefit is not netted against cost, so there is no payback figure.
3. The owner signs off their own assessment. The sign-off is a record that they read and decided, not a check by anyone else. The fingerprint detects a change to what it covers (the assessment's result and the standing decisions with their notes) made outside the application; it does not cover who decided or when, the sign-off's own note, or earlier decisions that were replaced, so a change to those goes unseen. It does not stop a change either (SQLite cannot stop `DROP TRIGGER`), and nothing stores the fingerprint anywhere else. Covering the whole event log would change the digest, so it would be a new version of the digest and not an edit.
4. A decision note is free text up to 500 characters; nothing reads it.
5. Making an assessment is cheap and idempotent, but each new confirmed version adds a row that can never be removed, and nothing limits how many versions an owner can confirm.
6. The guards test and the health check confirm that triggers exist by name. They do not run them; the integration tests prove behaviour with real SQL, one probe per rule, and a trigger with the right name and a weaker body would pass the name checks.
7. The page was run in Chromium only, against a local Worker. The inherited platform limits apply (1, 8, 9 and 11 in [the platform status](../platform/STATUS.md)).
