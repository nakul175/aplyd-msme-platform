# Factory module: status

Owner: the factory module. Paths: `modules/factory/`, `app/factory/`, `app/api/companies/[id]/factory/`, and migrations 0003 and 0004 in `drizzle/`. Last updated 5 October 2026 (the page rebuilt, slice 10a).

This is slice 2, Describe: the owner describes the factory by answering a questionnaire, and the platform keeps every save as a new version. Protected paths touched: data scope (a company's own record, read and written only by that company's owner) and migrations (a new append-only table). An independent reviewer has not yet read this slice, and a person has not read it ([VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md)).

## What works

- An owner opens `/factory`, sees the newest saved version filled in, and saves a change. Every save appends a version; nothing is edited or deleted. Earlier versions open read-only from the history list.
- Two kinds of save. A draft needs only one answer. "Confirm this is accurate" needs five answers (sector, products, employees, processes, problems); a form that asks to confirm without them is saved as a draft and the page names what is missing, so typed text is never lost. The JSON interface answers 400 for the same case.
- Stale saves are refused. Each form carries the version it started from. If another save landed first, nothing is written and the person is told (303 to the page for a form, 409 for JSON). The check and the insert are one SQL statement, so two simultaneous saves cannot both succeed.
- The scope rule is the platform's own: an owner reads and writes only their company. A request for another company's record, or from the operator, answers 404, identical to a record that does not exist, and is audited (`factory.read.denied`, `factory.write.denied`). A person who is signed in but not an owner gets 403. Every query is bound to the signed-in person's company, not to the id in the URL; a static test checks every SQL string on the table.
- Every created version writes an audit entry (`factory.revision.created`) in the same database batch, and only when the insert happened.
- `factory_revisions` refuses UPDATE, DELETE and any INSERT that would replace a row, whether it collides on its id or on (company, version), through three triggers. The health check reports `degraded` when one is missing. As with the audit log, `DROP TRIGGER`, `DROP TABLE` and `ALTER TABLE` cannot be stopped inside SQLite.
- Answers are validated against the questionnaire: unknown questions, wrong types, unlisted options, repeated options, more than three problems, over-long text and control characters are refused, with a message per question. A body over 64 KB is refused (413) without being read to its end (the body is taken in pieces and abandoned once it passes four bytes for each character allowed; a false declared length does not get round it); content types other than JSON and form posts are refused (415) before the body is read. A note is at most 500 characters and has no control characters or text-direction overrides, as the answers do.
- A form that cannot be saved goes back to `/factory` with a message and, for a refused answer, the names of the questions (taken from the questionnaire, sorted, never what was sent); a program is answered with JSON as before. A field a form carries that the questionnaire does not have is not read at all.
- A revision's note is at most 500 characters and 2,000 bytes in the table (SQLite's `length()` stops counting at a NUL character, so the bytes bound is what stops a note that hides its length behind one), and its answers must be stored as text that is a JSON object (the same migration; a BLOB holding `{}` is refused: it passed `json_valid` and could not be read back). A company may save at most 500 versions (an assumption, in the code and in a trigger of migration 0014): the 501st is refused with 409 `too_many_revisions` (a form is sent back with a message) and nothing is added. The history lists the newest 100 versions and says how many there are (`revisionsSaved`); older ones are kept and open by number. The table also holds, by trigger, that the answers are a JSON object, that revision numbers have no gaps, and that a note is at most 500 characters.
- Company A has one confirmed SYNTHETIC version; company B has none, so both an existing record and an empty one can be shown.
- Unit, integration, static, smoke and browser tests, and mutation checks ([VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md)).

## The page after the interface rebuild (slice 10a)

`/factory` ("Describe your factory") was rebuilt on the shared interface of slice 10 (`app/globals.css`, `app/_ui/`, [the interface guide](../../docs/design/INTERFACE-GUIDE.md)). Behaviour did not change: the same form posts the same fields (`a.<question>`, `note`, `intent`, the hidden `based_on`) to the same endpoint, the redirect keys (`?saved=`, `?confirmed=`, `?missing=`, `?stale=`, `?rev=`, `?problem=`, `?fields=`) and the messages are the same, and the labels, ids, `data-testid`s and notice roles that the tests find the page by are the same. What changed is what a person sees.

- **The top of the page** shows the five steps (Describe underlined), one sentence on what the page is, and one card on where the description stands: a tag (Confirmed, Draft or Not started), the sentence "Newest version: N, confirmed as accurate (or a draft), saved DATE." (or "Nothing saved yet."), a bar of "N of M questions answered in the newest saved version", and one line about the five questions needed to confirm ("2 of 5 questions needed to confirm are answered." or "All 5 ... You can confirm this version."; nothing once the newest version is confirmed).
- **The questions** are five numbered cards, one per box of the questionnaire, each a group of questions with a label, a help line and the control. The five questions needed to confirm carry a small "Needed to confirm" tag at the start of their help line (not in the label); State and District sit side by side on a wide screen; an option list of more than five is drawn in two columns on a wide screen; a group of choices is a real `fieldset` with a `legend`.
- **The two buttons** are in a bar stuck to the bottom of the screen: "Save as draft" (plain) and "Confirm this is accurate" (the one main button). The optional note ("What changed since last time?") and the sentence about what confirming means sit in a card just above the bar. On a short screen the bar is not stuck, so it never hides the questions.
- **Earlier versions** are a collapsed section (open when a past version is being looked at), one row per version with its confirmed or draft tag, its date and its note; the version being looked at is tagged "Shown above". A past version is read-only as before (dashed fields, disabled choices, no buttons, the same notice).
- **The count** comes from `src/progress.ts` (`progressOf`, `progressWords`, `confirmWords`), a pure function of the saved answers of the newest version, worked out on the server, so it does not move while the person types (the words say "in the newest saved version"). It counts questions of the questionnaire only. Two questions are follow-ups ("If something else, what?" for the sector, and for the problems): they are counted only once the answer that asks for them is given, so a complete description reads "23 of 23", not "23 of 25", and a count never needs a target that is not in the questionnaire. This is a choice made for the page, written in the code and tested; it is not a rule of the questionnaire (the server still accepts both when given).
- **No script runs on the page.** It is server-rendered with a plain form. It has its own small stylesheet (`app/factory/factory.css`, every class prefixed `fc-`).

## Not built

- Voice and photos, and the owner's own language. The description is typed, in English, as a form. The thesis describes this step as voice, photos and a few questions, in the owner's own language ([thesis.md](../../docs/thesis.md), section on the journey); this slice builds only "a few questions". It is where the slice is most likely to be wrong about how owners will actually describe a factory.
- Turning free text into structured answers (an AI step). Nothing here calls a model.
- An advisor or consultant role. Only the company's owner can read or write. Diagnose (slice 3) needs a scoped way for APLYD staff to read a company's record, which is a data-scope change and gets its own review.
- Withdrawing or erasing a description. Versions are permanent by design, so a request to remove personal data would need a decision first (OQ-07).
- Comparing two versions, or showing who changed what field.
- Merging after a stale save. The person sees the newest version and retypes.

## Known limits

1. The questionnaire's questions and option lists are assumptions, not findings. The four sectors follow the study's four sectors (source S4 in [sources.md](../../docs/sources.md)) plus "something else"; size bands, budget bands and the list of problems are round numbers and guesses. They are marked as assumptions in the code. OQ-03 (first sector and cluster) decides what they should become. A questionnaire change is a new `QUESTIONNAIRE_VERSION`; old versions keep the version they were saved with.
2. Nothing tests the questionnaire with a real owner. The ten-owner test (thesis section 7) has not been run.
3. A user who has saved a version cannot be deleted (a foreign key protects the history). There is no `disabled` flag on users yet.
4. Only owners write. A company with several people has one author per version, recorded in `created_by`; there is no co-author or approval.
5. The page is server-rendered with a plain form post. It has no client-side checks beyond the browser's own `maxlength`; the server is the check. It was run in one browser engine (Chromium), see the platform limit 11.
6. The browser tests were run only against the local Worker, not against a hosted environment.
7. The inherited platform limits apply, in particular 1 (synthetic sign-in has no password), 8 (an authenticated person can flood the audit log with denied requests) and 9 (the baseline can be edited by hand).

## Update, 6 October 2026: polish (slice 11d)

- **The page has its own title**, "Describe: APLYD" (a visitor sees the same title), from a template in `app/layout.tsx` that every page uses.
- **Dates are written the way an owner says them**: "Newest version: 2, a draft, saved 4 October 2026", and the same in the list of earlier versions and in the notice above an old version. They come from `formatDate` in `app/_ui/dates.ts`, which writes the UTC date of a timestamp. Anything that is not a real date comes back unchanged.
- **A Save pressed after the session ended** goes to the sign-in page with "Your session has ended. Sign in again; what you typed was not saved." and nothing is written (platform STATUS, "a form post with no valid session"). The two answers of `handleRevisionCreate` and `handleFactoryRead` for a missing session come from `notSignedIn(request)`; a program call still gets 401 JSON.
- **The numbers on the sections** are decoration: the style sheet gives them an empty alternative text (`content: counter(fcs) / ""`), so a browser that reads generated content should read a heading once and not as "1 About your unit". Not tried with a screen reader.
- **The empty page says what will appear and what causes it** (BR-UX-07): "Nothing saved yet.", "A draft can be saved after answering just one question.", and in the list of earlier versions "No earlier versions yet. Your first save will be version 1." The words were already there; a smoke test (`demo/smoke/interface`) now pins them for an owner with nothing saved.
- **Limits added.** The date is the UTC date: a description saved between midnight and 05:30 in India shows the day before. Whether to show Indian time is a decision for the owner of the interface guide; `formatDate` is the one place to change.
